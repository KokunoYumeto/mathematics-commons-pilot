# Mathematics Commons project logbook

This is the durable chronological memory of the concept and publication work. Append; do not silently rewrite prior entries. Corrections should identify the earlier entry they supersede.

## 2026-08-06 — Origin and project thesis

- The project arose from discussion among moderators and contributors of an AI mathematics community considering a shared GitHub for community conjectures.
- The scope was deliberately expanded beyond one subreddit or one conjecture. Established open problems, especially selected Erdős problems, provide better calibration targets and stronger prior literature.
- The core proposal became a decentralized, AI-integrated mathematics workflow: literature review, exact statement work, proof and counterexample search, computation, formalization, independent review, synthesis, and publication.
- Working project name: **The Mathematics Commons**.
- Working protocol name: **peer-to-peer review**. This is the project's name for version-bound review across independently operated human–AI nodes. No claim is made here that the phrase has no prior uses elsewhere.
- Core rule: model agreement is not truth. Claims advance through exact statements, evidence, provenance, independent checking, and human responsibility.

## 2026-08-06 — Inclusion and contribution integrability

- Two concepts were separated:
  - accessibility and inclusion across language, disability, geography, cost, technical access, and corpus bias;
  - broad integrability of many useful contribution types into one durable workflow.
- The project evaluates artifacts and evidence rather than credentials, biography, compute ownership, model tier, or a generalized intelligence ranking.
- Expertise remains necessary for advanced review but is applied claim by claim rather than treated as a personal caste.
- The workflow's granular source, proof, computation, formalization, translation, review, correction, and archival roles naturally weaken an isolated-genius narrative.
- “Folding@home for mathematics” is retained as a useful participation metaphor and a possible future compute layer, not a description of the current architecture.

## 2026-08-06 — Lessons from active corpus workflows

- Representative active Deligne, FGA, EGA, SGA, Noether, translation, Stacks-scaffold, and Zenodo tasks were inspected.
- Reusable practices include exact authority sources, hashes, page-bounded work, no-overlap ownership, diplomatic versus corrected layers, stable semantic IDs, append-only correction/reversal ledgers, independent checker lanes, build and visual QA, immutable releases, and archival readback.
- The active EGA work demonstrated a provisional integration scaffold with explicit non-official status, stable metadata units, collision records, and reviewable feedback hooks.
- These workflows showed that source recovery, transcription, translation, correction, semantic integration, and release work can be peer-to-peer packets, not merely theorem proving.
- The inter-language work is a source of design lessons and a possible later project-tree variant. It is not the current pilot.

## 2026-08-06 — Leiden Declaration becomes leading framework

- The complete official Leiden Declaration of 2 June 2026 was read wholesale, not only its individual-author recommendations.
- It was interpreted as enabling responsible AI-assisted mathematics: do mathematics well rather than stop doing it.
- The workflow now covers proof and understanding; human attribution, authorship, and responsibility; transparency and independent verification; proper evaluation of depth, difficulty, and significance; expert judgment; research autonomy; care for people and environment; review burden; open science; tool disclosure; proactive attribution; public discourse; new contributors; tool choice; ethics; author rights and training consent; appropriate publication; public infrastructure; industry asymmetry; funding; anti-hype communication; regulation; and freedom of conscience.
- Important precision: the International Mathematical Union endorsed the Declaration, not this project. The Commons may say it is designed to align with Leiden, self-assessed; it may not claim certification or endorsement.
- Formal verification is used where feasible and appropriate. It does not replace a human check that the formal statement matches the intended theorem.
- Internal peer-to-peer review does not replace journals or broader mathematical scrutiny.
- A clause-level traceability document was created so Leiden cannot become a decorative citation.

## 2026-08-06 — Current pilot versus later scope

- Current goal was narrowed to a grassroots GitHub-native pilot for the initial contributors already familiar with AI agents.
- The first lanes are selected Erdős or comparable established open problems and carefully stated community conjectures.
- Initial packet types are literature/open-status audit, citation checking, proof or lemma attempt, counterexample search, exact computation, exposition, formalization, and adversarial review.
- Approximately six to ten records are recommended, including an uncertain-status calibration item and a deliberately flawed proof to test the review system.
- The pilot succeeds by producing durable reviewable work and catching errors; solving an open problem is not required.
- Broad non-Git onboarding, polished one-click compute donation, archive/transcription trees, global translation, and genuinely local peer-to-peer inference were moved to later phases.

## 2026-08-06 — GitHub technical decision

- Git itself already distributes complete repository history. GitHub is used initially as a visible rendezvous point, not the only scientific record.
- Start with one public organization/repository, not one repository per conjecture.
- Contributors use forks or clones, one branch per bounded packet, pull requests, independent review files, and protected integration into `main`.
- Issues and Projects coordinate work but do not hold the only durable scientific state because ordinary Git mirrors do not preserve them.
- Use one-way bare mirrors during the pilot, immutable releases, checksum manifests, and Zenodo archival deposits.
- Use GitHub-hosted runners with read-only permissions for untrusted public contributions. Never expose a persistent personal runner or secrets to arbitrary pull-request code.
- A truly peer-to-peer forge such as Radicle or federated-forge technology may be evaluated later; it is unnecessary for the proof of concept.

## 2026-08-06 — Capability and packet routing

- Subscription price and model tier are transient interface facts, not durable scientific metadata or authority.
- Durable envelopes are `light`, `standard`, `extended`, and `specialized`, based on actual time, budget, context, hardware, tools, languages, rights, and security requirements.
- A small subscription receives a smaller coherent packet, not a lower evidentiary standard. A larger subscription receives larger feasible scope, not more credit or voting power.
- Manual labels and steward assignment are enough for the first pilot. Automatic routing and a polished Node Kit follow later.

## 2026-08-06 — Translation and marginal intelligibility

- A future translation program may index broad preprint and historical corpora and prioritize translations by **marginal intelligibility gain**: expected additional mathematical access per unit of translation and review effort.
- Inputs include unmet language need, foundational reach, pedagogy, community demand, source authority, rights feasibility, and reviewer capacity. Citation count is only one signal because it can reproduce existing corpus bias.
- Language packs should contain source authority, terminology and notation guidance, packet JSON, build templates, and independent review instructions.
- arXiv and other preprint servers may be indexed, but public accessibility is not blanket permission to translate and redistribute. Compatible licenses or author permission are required. Old works and modern scans/editions require separate rights checks.
- This is a future project tree, not part of the current conjecture pilot.

## 2026-08-06 — Commons rights and academic authorship

- Final project rule: **CC0 for all Commons-originated material, modulo pre-existing third-party copyright and other rights.**
- Commons-originated includes every new component created and submitted through the workflow: candidate arguments, proofs, counterexamples, computations, formalizations, code, metadata, reviews, corrections, transcriptions, translations, exposition, indexes, and documentation.
- If purely machine-determined expression has no copyright, CC0 confirms the intended commons status. If a human may hold rights through selection, arrangement, editing, or another contribution, participation dedicates those rights under CC0 to the fullest extent possible.
- The project does not rely on a universal claim that every jurisdiction treats all AI-assisted work identically. The contribution covenant produces the open result regardless.
- Third-party rights are not erased by AI processing or CC0. Every incorporated source must have provenance, license/rights, transformation, training-consent, and redistribution fields.
- Academic authorship is separate from copyright. Human authorship, priority, credit, explanation, and responsibility are governed by Leiden and scholarly norms. AI systems are disclosed as tools and are not authors.
- Prizes, employment, reputation, and academic credit do not depend on copyright ownership.
- Later scholarly publication may use any appropriate peer-reviewed venue compatible with continued CC0 availability and full disclosure. Contributors may not grant exclusive control over the Commons artifact or remove its public record.

## 2026-08-06 — Prompt-disclosure and module-privacy correction

- A complete exact-text audit of the official Leiden Declaration confirmed that “proprietary prompts” is not Declaration wording. Leiden asks for transparent disclosure of material tool use, information needed for scientific evaluation, reviewer support, and mathematical arguments that can be independently understood without proprietary knowledge or equipment. It does not require publication of prompts, transcripts, chain-of-thought, private notes, or personal data.
- The white paper and normative requirements now begin their Leiden treatment with a privacy presumption: ordinary AI interactions are private by default, submitting an artifact is not consent to publish the interaction history, and raw interaction records are optional and contributor-controlled after privacy, confidentiality, security, and rights review.
- Proportionate public disclosure now consists of a sanitized functional task specification, material tools and human interventions, supporting sources and artifacts, checks, limitations, and relevant disclosure gaps. A prompt is recorded only as process provenance where voluntarily publishable; it is not proof, authorship, or a sufficient explanation of theorem-level work.
- Mathematical claims must stand on independently checkable public arguments and evidence. Empirical claims about model autonomy or performance may require more protocol detail, but unavailable private detail narrows the claim rather than creating a right to compel disclosure.
- Privacy is enforced module by module through allowlisted inputs and destinations, public/private/restricted/temporary/prohibited classifications, retention and redaction rules, and an exact pre-publication bundle preview. Contribution, agent, rights, technical-guide, status, release-note, and recovery texts were updated consistently.
