# Target-language selection

Choose one exact language and record its locale, script, and orthographic standard. A broad label such as “Chinese,” “Arabic,” or “Serbo-Croatian” is insufficient when the intended edition depends on region, script, or standard.

The current interlanguage discovery snapshot records work-specific coverage in Arabic, German, English, Spanish, Persian, French, Indonesian, Interslavic, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Sanskrit, Ukrainian, Vietnamese, multiple Chinese conventions, and several mixed or undetermined records. These tags do not mean that every work exists in those languages. Check the selected work's row before treating a language as covered.

No authoritative UNESCO list of “96 underserved languages” was identified for this starter. Do not attach that label to an invented list. UNESCO does report that many learners lack education in a language they speak or understand. Language priority should therefore be justified with work-specific educational need, community input, existing-material coverage, and local review capacity.

A valid target may be any language not already maintained for the selected work. Declared independent overlap is also allowed when it has a clear review or edition purpose.
